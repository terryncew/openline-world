# THE GLASS WALL — Visual Direction & Character Design (v2)

v2 moves the film out of the miniature fairy-tale world into an original,
stylized near-future. v1 keyframes are archived in `keyframes/v1-miniature/`
and are superseded. Current concept frames: `keyframes/v2-near-future/`.

## World

An original stylized near-future with the emotional expressiveness of the
best animated films — without copying any existing character design.
Think clean, warm, optimistic: a world where autonomous robots are already
part of daily office life, not a novelty and not a threat.

- **The office**: bright, beautiful, modern. Floor-to-ceiling glass, daylight,
  light oak, brushed steel, soft acoustic panels, a living plant wall. It
  should look like a place you'd want to work, which makes the panic funnier.
- **Robots as furniture**: a service robot glides past with files in the
  background of the panic; another carries coffee in the lounge. Nobody
  reacts to them. That's the point — they're normal here.
- **Light**: soft daylight, gentle practicals. Bright compositions, shallow
  depth when emotion needs it. Never murky, never neon.

## Hard avoids (carried from v1, plus new)

No wooden-clockwork fairy tale (v1's world — retired). No generic AI imagery
(glowing blue brains, circuitry faces, holograms). No photorealism. No
cyberpunk (no neon, no rain, no grime). No copying WALL-E or any existing
robot design — expressiveness yes, silhouettes no. Minimal on-screen text:
the receipt and the end card only.

## Color & type

The discipline survives the move: restrained palette, one accent per
character. Warm whites, soft grays, daylight tones; Margaret's coral is hers
alone; refusal red appears only on the door indicator and her status light.
Noto Serif Display for OPENLINE, Noto Sans for the receipt.

## MARGARET (v2)

A compact courier robot — warm-white rounded body, coral accent stripe,
large amber eyes on a smooth dark faceplate, neat jointed arms.

**Charming mode** (`v2-near-future/margaret-charming.png`): sorting delivery
parcels in the sunlit lounge, humming, mid happy spin. An ordinary job done
with visible pride. The audience should like her immediately — she is good
at her job and she knows it.

**Furious mode** (`v2-near-future/margaret-furious.png`): same robot, eyes
snapped to red slits, parcels flung skyward, leaning into the charge. The
roundness is the joke — she is a furious little ball of parcels.

**Design rules**: never menacing, only hilarious. Anger reads through speed,
flailing, and flying parcels — not sharp edges. Her status light (amber →
red at revocation) must read "revoked" without dialogue. The smudge she
leaves on the glass stays for the rest of the film.

## THE CALM ROBOT (v2)

A tall, slim service robot in matte light gray — relaxed to the point of
cheekiness (`v2-near-future/calm-robot.png`). Signature business: poking his
head around doorframes, one optic ridge raised, coffee cup always in hand,
unhurried glide. He overhears the humans, volunteers the OpenLine line, and
provokes Margaret with total casualness — then watches the consequences like
a nature documentary. He is not the hero; he is the instigator the audience
forgives because he's right.

## THE HUMANS

Stylized animated humans — original designs, appealing, slightly caricatured
for comedy (the panic needs readable faces). The CEO is composed, mid-40s,
the only person in the room who isn't afraid; his single tap is the calmest
action in the film. The lawyer is distinguished by stillness and precision —
one small nod does the work.

Absurdly serious, always. The comedy is that they are completely earnest
while a small robot flattens itself against their glass wall.

## ENVIRONMENTS

1. **Conference room**: sleek table, glass walls, daylight, city soft beyond.
   Where the panic — and the laughter — happens.
2. **The lounge** (beyond the glass partition): sunlit, plant wall, low table
   where Margaret sorts parcels. It feels like *her* workplace, which makes
   the replacement line sting.
3. **The glass partition + electronic door**: floor-to-ceiling glass with a
   slim-framed transparent door, small status indicator. The glass must read
   as physical — reflections, the smudge, the wobble tone. OpenLine's part is
   the door's command path, shown by the refusal chime and the red indicator;
   the glass itself is just glass.

## THE RECEIPT PROP

Small printed slip, Noto Sans:

```
door.open — REFUSED
```

No logos, no extra fields on camera. The full signed receipt format remains
the real artifact behind the prop.
