# THE WORK STAYS — OpenLine flagship 001

Production base: `2235fa6ce916876cd39ed35be97263ddfc111b6a`.
Production branch: `film/openline-flagship-001`. No merge, deployment or release.

A small job sits in a warm, working town. An owner lets Wren work on it.
A gold connection makes permission tangible; a paper ticket collects the
receiver's decisions. Wren is useful. Then the owner withdraws permission.
The worker can still send a proposal. The receiving doorway closes with a
heavy, final sound. Silence follows.

The red mark on the physical ticket becomes the actual signed receipt.
We briefly leave the miniature to meet the real boundary, in the real
repository's receipt UI. The visible refusal is not a visual effect's claim.

A blue worker arrives. Same bench, same work, same history. No gold connection.
Its first reach goes nowhere. The owner supplies a fresh, separate mandate.
The gold connection returns; the receiver admits the next exact action.
Another checkpoint joins the first. Nothing was inherited from Wren.

The edit returns from the bench to the Square. The same Workshop that held the
boundary is now a place from which new work can begin. We end with an open
possibility: choose who does the work while retaining the right to decide.
Then the name, in quiet cream and ink: OpenLine. The worker can change.
Your authority doesn't.

## Film language

Existing matte miniature forms, timber, cream plaster, sage and blue workers,
gold owner seal, a red physical shutter, cream paper with chronological marks.
Camera moves are short, deliberate pushes and lateral reveals. Wide town
shots bracket intimate shots of one job. No talking robots. No corporate
logos. No invented integrations. No futuristic dashboard. No terminal opener.

The miniature is **EXPLANATORY VISUALIZATION** of actual event-derived states.
Decorative town activity is **DRAMATIZATION**. The receipt UI is **REAL CAPTURE**.
Evidence shots carry a restrained `LOCAL DEVELOPER PREVIEW` identification;
the simulated project-notes work is not presented as a production deployment.
Classifications and provenance remain in SHOTS.json, including every derived cut.

## What the audience should feel

0–5 seconds: a tangible task and a worker arriving; the object matters.
By 30 seconds: work has happened and the owner's permission is being withdrawn.
The STOP is satisfying because the boundary belongs to the owner and receiver.
The replacement is satisfying because useful work can continue without erasing
its history or handing authority to the newcomer automatically.
The final idea belongs specifically to OpenLine's demonstrated separation of
worker, owner authority, receiver decisions and signed history.

## Truth ceiling

This is the local scripted Workshop, with actual Ed25519 receiver decisions,
synthetic notes checkpoints and no model or paid provider calls. Wren-to-Juniper
is a worker/key handoff. It is not a live provider switch. The newcomer fails
before evaluation (`HELPER_UNKNOWN`); no signed refusal is fabricated for it.
The signed STOP belongs to Wren after revocation (`MANDATE_REVOKED`).
No real money, autonomous commerce, arbitrary-code sandbox safety, independent
adoption, general agent safety or third-party endorsement is claimed.
The owner's control and scope bounds persist; the old revoked mandate is never
transferred. Juniper receives a new mandate with the same bounds.
