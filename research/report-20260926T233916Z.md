## Interpretation
The following is model interpretation, not sourced fact. The sources suggest that an agent's identity is tied to its unique identifier (like a DID), which is controlled by the entity itself, while authority refers to the permissions and historical records of actions that can be verified independently of the agent's identity. This implies that identity is about who the agent is, while authority is about what the agent is allowed to do. The OpenLine Wallet framework indicates that an agent's authority can persist even if the agent's identity changes or if the agent is replaced. This suggests a model where the authority to act is not inherently tied to the identity of the agent performing the action. The OAuth 2.0 framework emphasizes the need for an external authorization server to grant access, indicating that authority is often mediated by external systems, while identity can be self-managed through mechanisms like DIDs.
