# BUILD-001 evaluator shortlist

Status: research only. Nobody has been contacted, nobody has agreed.
Public information only. JUN is deliberately excluded — his invitation
is held separately for the pilot and must not be conflated with this.

## 1. Simon Willison

- Public affiliation: independent; co-creator of Django, creator of
  Datasette (open source).
- Why he fits: the most consistent public practitioner-evaluator of AI
  agent tooling — reverse-engineers agent harnesses, publishes
  hands-on evaluations of model releases and agent products at
  simonwillison.net, coined "prompt injection" as a term of art.
  Comfortable running other people's code and judging it in public.
- Public contact route (found, not used): simonwillison.net (blog),
  github.com/simonw.
- Conflict: none known. Unlikely to field a team.

## 2. Hamel Husain

- Public affiliation: founder of Parlance Labs; ex-Airbnb, ex-GitHub.
- Why he fits: the clearest public voice on evaluation methodology for
  AI systems — "Evals Skills for Coding Agents", the Evals FAQ at
  hamel.dev, co-author of the O'Reilly book "Evals for AI Engineers".
  His whole method is error analysis before metrics, which matches a
  frozen-checks-plus-usability-task design.
- Public contact route (found, not used): hamel.dev (blog),
  github.com/hamelhusain.
- Conflict: none known. Unlikely to field a team.

## 3. Shreya Shankar

- Public affiliation: UC Berkeley CS PhD; co-author of the O'Reilly
  book "Evals for AI Engineers" with Hamel Husain.
- Why she fits: research on LLM-as-judge alignment with human
  preferences ("Who Validates the Validators?", UIST 2024) and data
  quality assertions (SPADE, VLDB 2024). The usability-task half of
  the evaluation is her home turf.
- Public contact route (found, not used): sh-reya.com,
  github.com/shreyashankar.
- Conflict / risk: joining Carnegie Mellon as an assistant professor
  in 2027 — availability is a real question. No team conflict.

## 4. Michael Niedermayer

- Public affiliation: FFmpeg project lead 2004–2015, release manager;
  still active on ffmpeg-devel.
- Why he fits: the deepest multimedia credibility available — the
  frozen objective checks are all about codec, container, timing, and
  filter correctness, which is exactly his domain. Would spot a
  bogus "valid MP4" claim instantly.
- Public contact route (found, not used): ffmpeg-devel mailing list;
  michael@niedermayer.cc (published on patchwork.ffmpeg.org).
- Conflict: none for fielding a team. Note: historically polarizing
  figure (the Libav fork); credibility here is strictly technical.

## Deliberately not listed

- Dan Dennedy (Shotcut/MLT): credible on video tooling, but more
  plausibly fields a team than judges one — conflict.
- JUN (OpenCodex): held separately; do not approach under BUILD-001.
