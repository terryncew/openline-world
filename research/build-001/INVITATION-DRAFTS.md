## Draft A — evaluator

Subject: Invitation to independently evaluate a small open-source video tool event

Hello [name],

I am organizing BUILD-001, a 72-hour open-source event. Three
independent teams each build a tool that turns a horizontal screen
recording plus a timed transcript into a vertical 9:16 clip: a human
selects moments, adjusts framing and captions, and exports a normal
720x1280 MP4. All rendering happens on each team's own machine. The
task is fixed and public; teams are scored on a hidden clip they
never see.

I am looking for an independent evaluator — someone with no stake in
any team — to do the scoring. The commitment is roughly a day of
work, after the 72 hours end: build each of the three entries from
source, run the frozen objective checks (dimensions, caption timing
and placement, protected regions, audio sync, file validity), carry
out a short usability task with the fixed script on the hidden clip,
and publish the results. The checks and the rubric are frozen and
public before the event starts, so there is nothing to invent.

Recognition, not money: there is no prize money. Entries are judged
pass/fail on the frozen checks; among passing entries a published
rubric selects Best Usable Tool, with ties or joint winners allowed.
Every contribution included in a released build gets recorded credit.
If no entry passes, there is no winner and the findings are published
honestly anyway.

Costs: none to you beyond your time. The evaluation runs on your own
machine in a disposable environment we define together; you never
touch our live systems or credentials.

If you are interested, I will send the frozen evaluation rules and
the rubric for your review before you commit. No reply is also fine.

— Terrynce White

## Draft B — participant team

Subject: Invitation to BUILD-001: a 72-hour open-source clip-tool event

Hello [name/handle],

I am organizing BUILD-001, a 72-hour open-source event, and I would
like to invite you to field a team. The task: build a usable
open-source tool that turns a horizontal screen recording plus a
supplied timed transcript into a vertical clip. A human selects the
moments, adjusts the framing and captions, and exports a normal
720x1280 MP4 with readable captions that avoid designated protected
regions. All rendering happens on your own machines. A public
practice clip is provided up front; scoring uses a different hidden
clip you will never see, under the same disclosed requirements.

The shape of the 72 hours: 48 hours of separate development, then a
hard freeze of your entry and its dependencies. The final 24 hours are
optional: teams may share, review, and integrate each other's work,
but only with explicit permission, and every reuse is recorded with
attribution. Before the clock starts, each team completes a short
submission rehearsal so the mechanics are proven. The clock starts
only after three approved teams have finished onboarding.

Recognition, not money: there is no prize money and no entry fee.
Entries are judged pass/fail against frozen objective checks plus a
short usability task. Among passing entries, a published rubric
selects Best Usable Tool — ties and joint winners are allowed, and if
nothing passes there is no winner. Credits for contributions actually
included in a released build are recorded in the source and the
release notes.

Costs: your own compute, bounded and disclosed by you. We spend
nothing on participants and ask for nothing beyond the entry.

This is an experiment in whether separately operated teams can build
and integrate a usable artifact with traceable attribution. We will
report what happened descriptively, before and after, without
claiming the format caused any improvement.

If you want in, reply and I will send the frozen rules, the practice
clip, and the rehearsal steps. No reply is also fine.

— Terrynce White
